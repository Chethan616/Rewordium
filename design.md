## Brand & Style

Rewordium is a productivity-focused writing assistant with a friendly, modern tone. The visual language balances trust and energy: soft neutral surfaces keep long text sessions calm, while vivid accents communicate action and confidence.

The overall identity is practical rather than decorative. UI chrome stays light and low-noise so content and generated writing remain the center of attention. Rounded geometry, lightweight shadows, and clear hierarchy make interactions feel approachable across fast utility flows such as login, rewrite, grammar checks, and keyboard actions.

## Colors

The palette is anchored by mint-teal primary and deep navy secondary with warm amber tertiary support.

- Primary color drives confirmation and main CTAs.
- Secondary color supports navigation emphasis and trusted utility actions.
- Tertiary color is used for optional highlights and warm attention points.
- Surface layers are cool off-whites and blue-grays, creating depth without heavy contrast jumps.
- Dark mode keeps readable near-black layers with bright key accents, preserving the same semantic mapping.

System status colors are clear and direct: red for errors, amber for warnings, and green for success states.

Keyboard visuals use a separate but consistent tonal set: neutral key beds with a saturated blue accent for active controls.

## Typography

Typography is system-native sans-serif for most UI text to maximize platform familiarity and legibility. The scale follows a practical hierarchy:

- Display and headline levels are bold and slightly tighter for strong wayfinding.
- Body levels are straightforward and readable for sustained writing workflows.
- Label styles are denser and semibold to support controls, chips, and compact metadata.

A single script wordmark style is reserved for splash branding. It should not be used for body content, controls, or dense interfaces.

## Layout & Spacing

The layout system is compact-mobile first, centered on a 4px base unit with common jumps at 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, and 32.

- Primary screen padding is 16px.
- Modal and sheet interiors use 20px horizontal rhythm.
- Controls target comfortable touch dimensions near 48 to 56px.
- Floating navigation and tool surfaces rely on generous corner radii and medium internal padding for tap clarity.

The interface should feel breathable, but not sparse. Prioritize efficient scanning and fast action loops.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is subtle and structural. Most core Material surfaces are flat at elevation 0, with separation introduced through tonal layers and restrained shadows.

- Cards rely on soft low-opacity shadows for separation from background.
- Floating navigation and sheet elements use stronger but still diffuse shadows.
- FAB states use explicit stepped elevation: rest, hover/focus, and pressed.
- Keyboard surfaces use compact key shadows to reinforce tactile affordance without visual clutter.

## Shapes

Shape language combines expressive large radii for primary surfaces with tighter square radii for utility controls.

- Large containers and hero cards trend toward 20 to 30px corners.
- Standard controls and inputs use 8 to 16px corners.
- Pills and indicators use fully rounded capsules.
- Material 3 Expressive round and square scales should remain consistent across app and component states.

The result should feel soft and modern while retaining precision in high-density tool panels.

## Components

Core component behavior:

- Primary buttons are filled teal with white text, bold label style, and high-radius silhouettes.
- Secondary buttons are outlined or low-emphasis surfaces that preserve clear affordance.
- Inputs are filled, low-contrast containers with distinct focused outline treatment.
- Cards are low-elevation blocks with strong rounding and readable text contrast.
- Floating nav bars are pill-like, elevated, and always readable over content.
- FABs sit above content with distinct container coloration and expressive radii.
- Snackbar and chip styles rely on inverse or container tones for quick status communication.
- Tool cards use saturated category colors while maintaining consistent sizing and shape.
- Keyboard components preserve larger type and tight spacing for high-speed entry.

## Do's and Don'ts

Do:

- Keep semantic color mapping stable between light and dark themes.
- Preserve rounded hierarchy so surfaces and controls remain visually coherent.
- Use motion durations consistently by interaction intent: micro, standard, and entrance tiers.
- Favor tonal separation before adding stronger shadows.

Don't:

- Replace primary/secondary semantics with arbitrary decorative colors.
- Flatten all radii to a single value; the system depends on mixed round and square sets.
- Overuse script typography outside the splash/brand context.
- Introduce harsh, high-opacity shadows that make the UI feel heavy.
